Classical Scholarship

Hadith and its sciences

A hadith has a chain of transmitters, the isnād, and a report text, the matn. Scholars considered continuity, narrator reliability, anomalous wording and hidden defects. Biographical dictionaries preserve information about teachers, students, dates and reliability. Comparing routes of transmission can clarify why one reading or judgement differs from another.

Books and commentaries

Hadith collections
Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim collect reports and organize them in ways that reward attention to their chapter structures.
Creed and method
Read early works of ʿaqīdah alongside historical accounts of their authors and the questions they addressed. Ibn al-Ṣalāḥ’s Muqaddimah offers a later systematic treatment of hadith terminology.
Commentary and biography
Ibn Ḥajar’s Fatḥ al-Bārī, al-Nawawī’s commentary on Muslim and al-Dhahabī’s Siyar Aʿlām al-Nubalāʾ point toward different ways to read a collection or follow a scholarly life.

From codex to digital facsimile

A manuscript preserves a physical history of copying and reading. Compare colophons, marginal notes, scripts and folio order before treating different copies as interchangeable. Digitization improves access, while shelfmarks, page references and a record of the edition help readers verify quotations.

The Qur’an folio shown here is dated 1137 CE and uses eastern Kufic with some elements of naskh. Its catalog record gives the object number and the related colophon evidence. Princeton’s collection offers additional examples across the languages of the Islamic world.
