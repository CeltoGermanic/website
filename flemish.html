Flemish Heritage

Historical Flanders

The medieval County of Flanders was part of the Low Countries. Its borders shifted and its territory differed from that of the modern Flemish Region. Clothmaking helped cities such as Bruges and Ghent develop large urban markets. Bruges linked Flemish commerce to Hanseatic merchants and other North Sea networks.

Language, trade and identity

Flemish commonly refers to Dutch as used in Flanders and to regional varieties associated with it. Dutch and German both belong to the West Germanic language family. Historical Flanders also contained areas where Romance varieties were spoken. Linguistic kinship and sustained commercial contact are useful contexts for Flemish–German connections. Flemish identity has its own history in the Low Countries.

Movement across borders

Merchants, artisans and religious communities moved through and out of the Low Countries over several centuries. Trade brought Flemish and German-speaking communities into contact, while political and religious changes also shaped migration. These are broad historical patterns. They provide context for studying Flemish heritage in a wider European setting.
