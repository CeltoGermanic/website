Historical Interests

Europe from antiquity to the Middle Ages

Roman roads, cities and inscriptions illuminate imperial expansion and local life in Iberia. Later archaeology and written records raise different questions about late antique migrations, Germanic-speaking peoples and medieval societies. Political labels, burial customs, language and biological ancestry can each move at a different pace.

In the medieval north, the Hanseatic League connects my interest in Bremen and the Weser to a larger trade network. Flanders adds the history of textiles, urban institutions and North Sea exchange. In Iberia, borderlands and multilingual manuscripts show how cultural institutions operated across political frontiers.

Indigenous history and colonial encounters

I follow the histories of Indigenous peoples in southern Brazil and the Río de la Plata with particular attention to Charrúa history. Archaeological traces, colonial terms, demographic estimates and living community memory have different limits. Accounts of colonization should include displacement, coerced labour and Indigenous decisions and survival.

Islamic intellectual history

The caliphates provide a political chronology, while the history of Ahl al-Ḥadīth and the movement of scholars show how learning travelled. Manuscript catalogues make it possible to follow a text through successive copies and trace part of its reception.
