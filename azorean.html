Azorean Heritage

My maternal Portuguese ancestry is predominantly Azorean. The history of Desterro, now Florianópolis, offers regional context for that heritage.

Nine islands in the Atlantic

The Azores form three island groups. Flores and Corvo lie to the west. Faial, Pico, São Jorge, Graciosa and Terceira form the central group. São Miguel and Santa Maria lie to the east. The islands’ position mattered to Portuguese Atlantic navigation, and their ports connected island life with ocean trade and imperial shipping.

Nossa Senhora do Desterro

In 1748 the first group in the organized Azorean settlement of Santa Catarina arrived off Desterro. Further voyages brought island families to communities on and around the Ilha de Santa Catarina, and some settlers eventually moved farther south. These movements form part of the regional history of Azorean heritage in Brazil.

Portuguese Crown settlement took place on lands with longstanding Indigenous histories. The vocabulary of “settlement” describes one phase of occupation and should be read alongside that longer history.

Island practices in southern Brazil

Coastal fishing, vernacular building, religious festivals and bobbin lace help explain the distinctive Azorean presence in the region. These practices changed in Brazil as families adapted to local materials, economies and neighbours. The Casa dos Açores in Biguaçu offers a place to trace that living material culture.

Sources for Azorean migration

Public collections in Santa Catarina preserve land grants and passenger material from the colonial era. Read alongside records from the Azores, these sources help researchers distinguish regional settlement patterns from individual voyages.
