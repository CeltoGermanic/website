Galician and Portuguese

Galician and Portuguese histories meet in medieval lyric and diverge across later borders and institutions. I’m interested in how modern speakers use related forms, such as Galician noite and Portuguese noite, and how translation handles differences beneath apparent similarity.

Gothic and historical Germanic

Gothic is an attested East Germanic language. The Codex Argenteus preserves much of a Gothic translation of the Gospels in a sixth-century manuscript. Old English, Old High German and Old Norse provide other points of comparison for inflection, regular sound correspondences and inherited vocabulary.

In historical linguistics an asterisk can mark a reconstructed form, such as Proto-Germanic *fōt- in comparisons with English foot and Gothic fōtus. A reconstruction is inferred from evidence. A manuscript reading is attested evidence with its own spelling and transmission history.
