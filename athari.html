The Athari Creed

Al-ʿAqīdah al-Athariyyah names the creed I identify with. Its emphasis on revelation and transmitted reports shapes how I approach learning.

Revelation and the transmitted tradition

العقيدة الأثرية

The word athar can refer to a transmitted report. In an Athari approach, the Qur’an and authentic Sunnah provide the basis for belief, with particular attention to the understanding of the early Muslims. In questions about Allah’s attributes, a characteristic formulation affirms what revelation says while withholding claims about the modality, bilā kayf.

Ahl al-Ḥadīth

Ahl al-Ḥadīth designates scholars associated with transmitting, evaluating and drawing on hadith. Aḥmad ibn Ḥanbal is an important figure in the history of hadith and traditionalist theology. The label describes a historical scholarly orientation with regional and individual variation.

Classical debates

The ninth-century miḥnah under the Abbasids brought disputes about the createdness of the Qur’an into relation with state authority. Aḥmad ibn Ḥanbal’s resistance became a landmark in traditionalist memory. Later debates about divine attributes and the role of kalām, or dialectical theology, produced a wide body of argument and commentary.

For a reading path, Aḥmad’s attributed Uṣūl al-Sunnah, al-Barbahārī’s Sharḥ al-Sunnah and Ibn Qudāmah’s Lumʿat al-Iʿtiqād offer examples of different periods and forms of traditionalist creed writing. Editions, attributions and commentary deserve attention when reading them.
